## The story (plain version)

Rook sends a job to a responder's phone. The responder taps "yes" to take it.

In August we changed the rules. We gave people **60 seconds** to say yes
instead of **90**. Lots of people could not reach their phone in time. They
were on the stairs. They were putting on their boots. The job went to someone
else.

The computer also keeps a score for each responder. When someone **does not
answer in time**, the computer counts it the same as saying **"no"**. Their
score goes down. A low score means fewer jobs. Fewer jobs means fewer chances to
win the score back. And the score never gets better by itself.

Four responders got stuck in this trap: **Vesper, Farlight, The Undertow and
Meteor Mite**. They used to get about 12 jobs a week. Now they get about 3 or 4,
and they miss most of those, because the phone has been quiet for days. Their
handlers wrote in. Nobody wrote back.

Other responders picked up their jobs and got busier. Twice as many jobs now end
with **nobody** taking them.

The fix is likely to need three parts: give people more time, stop counting a
missed call as a "no", and give the four stuck responders a fresh start.

### Does the change to who gets pinged first contribute? (tested, not decided)

**Observed [DB]:**
- Pings to a responder in their usual area fell from 83% to 61% overall.
- Most of that is spill-over. In the four trapped responders' areas, the first ping went to the local responder 63% of the time before and **15%** after; neighbours covered instead.
- Leaving out callouts in those four areas, the other 12 responders' usual-area share was 86–93% each week before 4.2. It dipped to **75% in release week**, then went back to 79–85%.

**Inferred:**
- The ranking change may have caused a small, early shift.
- It doesn't explain why four responders starved; the miss penalty does.
- I can't fully separate the two without live ranking scores (see data gaps).

### Your hunch: verdict
1. **Back to 90 s: supported.** At 90 s, 2% of pings were missed and 90% of callouts filled within 28 s [DB]. *Not tested:* whether 90 is the best number, because there's no data on how long it takes to accept.
2. **A miss shouldn't count as a "no": supported** by the replay (inferred).
3. **Not enough on its own.** Add:
   - a **one-time score reset** for the responders hurt since 12 Aug;
   - a **countdown on the phone, plus an alert to the handler**;
   - a decision on the 2019 question: should a responder who *keeps* missing still lose rank? For example, count misses only after several in a row.
